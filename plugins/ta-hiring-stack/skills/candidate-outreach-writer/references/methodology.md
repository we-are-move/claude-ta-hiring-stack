## Part 1: The framework

Move's documented methodology for converting cold candidate outreach into engaged conversations with passive talent. 60+ talent partnerships, 1,000+ hires, 40 percent and above reply rates across engineering, GTM, and senior specialist roles.

Industry benchmark for cold candidate outreach sits at 10 to 15 percent reply rates. The gap is structural, not effort. Single-message outreach captures roughly 18 percent of available pipeline. The remaining 82 percent of replies come from touches 2, 3, and 4. Teams running single-touch sourcing are not running a campaign. They are running a lottery.

### The 4-touch architecture

Cadence: Day 1, Day 4 to 5, Day 9 to 10, Day 14 to 17. Each touch has a specific job. No touch repeats what another touch already did.

#### Touch 1: The Hook (Day 1)

Length: 80 to 120 words. Mobile-readable in 20 seconds.

- Identity acknowledgement (one specific anchor to their work)
- Role framing (company, role, one credibility anchor: revenue, scale, investor, mission)
- One specific scope or ownership detail that hints at the contrarian frame
- Soft ask, conversation-shaped. Never "apply here" or "send your CV"

#### Touch 2: The Contrarian Frame (Day 4 to 5)

Length: 100 to 140 words. The strongest single hook in any Move sequence.

The structural move:

> Most [modal version of this role] do [the typical experience]. This one is [the inversion].

Examples:

- "Most senior strategic AM seats run a portfolio. This one is a single named US partner."
- "Most Staff Engineer roles at this stage optimise systems someone else designed. This one is greenfield architecture for a $250B industry that has never been digitised."
- "Most AI roles polish prototypes. This one is production-first."

Open with "Looping back on..." or "Following up on the...". Never "just checking in".

#### Touch 3: Day-to-Day Reality (Day 9 to 10)

Length: 130 to 150 words. Hard ceiling. Do not exceed.

The proof beat. Earn trust by being concrete where every other recruiter is vague. Touch 3 lands the three or four highest-leverage fields, NOT all seven. Picking too many fields produces bloat that kills the read rate.

Priority order for inclusion:

1. Reporting line (named manager plus pedigree)
2. Team underneath (size and composition)
3. Cultural tempo with ONE specific example (decision speed, ship cadence, autonomy). Concrete, not adjectives.
4. One concrete proof point (recent ship, named customer, comp anchor)

Skip or defer: time horizon (usually implied), location and remote (in subject anchor), compensation (defer to call unless seniority demands it), next step (one line at end).

#### Touch 4: Why Now and Honest Close (Day 14 to 17)

Length: 100 to 130 words.

- Why now framing tied to a specific recent event (funding, launch, scaling phase)
- For senior roles: iteration zero hook ("the playbook does not exist yet. The person in this seat will write it.")
- For junior roles: velocity hook ("new hires own their pipeline within four weeks")
- Clear close signalling this is the last message
- Future door ("if timing changes, you are very welcome to reconnect")
- Feedback ask ("even a one-liner helps us get better")

### The LinkedIn DM

Separate output. 100 to 130 words. Four short paragraphs. NOT a 50-word stub.

- Paragraph 1 (around 25 words): opener with identity acknowledgement.
- Paragraph 2 (around 30 to 40 words): role context with credibility anchor. Enough substance to evaluate.
- Paragraph 3 (around 30 to 40 words): the contrarian wedge.
- Paragraph 4 (around 10 words): low-pressure close ("worth a 15-minute look?").

Voice: peer-to-peer, mobile-read. The DM should feel like a message you would send to a smart friend.

### Subject line system

Single structural template, applied to every email subject:

> [Company - Role] | [hook phrase] (credibility anchor)

Anchor type varies by context:

- Hot scale-ups: revenue, growth rate, investor list
- Premium senior roles: comp band or equity
- Early-stage: mission, stage, founder pedigree
- Engineering at scale: technical scope or stack credibility

Generate 3 subject line variants per touch. Use hyphens between Company and Role, never em or en dashes.

### Voice rules

- 90/10 ratio: 90 percent about the candidate, 10 percent about the company. Audit every message before output.
- No em dashes. Recognisable AI tell. Use commas, full stops, colons, or parentheses.
- No en dashes. Use "to" or "and" between numbers ("Day 4 to 5", not "Day 4-5").
- Hyphens are fine in compound words (AI-native, 4-touch, day-to-day).
- One ask per message. No multi-CTA.

### Linguistic moves to deploy

- "Looping back on..." as the follow-up opener
- "Most X are Y. This one is Z." (the contrarian frame)
- "Not X. This is Y." (anti-pattern naming)
- "In practice, this means..." (concretisation)
- "For someone joining at this stage, this means..." (perspective shift)

### Anti-patterns

- "Hope you are well" or any throat-clearing opener
- Apologetic framing ("sorry to bother you")
- Corporate buzzwords ("rocket ship", "passionate team", "exciting opportunity", "synergy")
- Generic role descriptions that could apply to any company
- Vague social proof ("similar candidates have found it interesting")
- Multi-CTA messages
- Touch 1 above 120 words. Touch 3 above 150 words. DM under 90 or above 140 words.
- Personalisation that signals surveillance (more than one specific detail beyond public info)

### Role-archetype switches

Progression framing:

- GTM: scope and ownership progression ("own your pipeline cold to qualified", "single named partner")
- Engineering: craft and identity progression ("make AI the whole job", "systems still being actively shaped")
- Product and senior leadership: influence and authoring progression ("you will author the playbook")

Compensation disclosure:

- Senior plus comfortable client: explicit range with structure split
- Senior plus reserved client: "competitive salary" in subject anchor, deferred in body
- Junior: defer ("happy to share the job description and compensation")

## Part 2: Reference campaigns

Three fully anonymised reference campaigns, compressed to the 4-step format. The companies, industries, locations, investors, and exec details below have been altered. Use these as few-shot grounding for voice, structure, and length, not as templates for the specifics. Original sequences achieved 40 percent and above reply rates.

### Reference A: Junior GTM (SDR, European B2B SaaS)

Sales Development Representative at a Series C European B2B SaaS platform serving finance and operations teams. Pan-European hub in Madrid. Candidate audience: 1 to 3 years outbound experience.

#### Touch 1: The Hook

Subject: [Company] - SDR | pan-European career platform (every European market, one floor)

Hi [First Name], I came across your LinkedIn profile, and it looks like outbound and pipeline work is your lane, which is why I wanted to flag the SDR seat we are filling at [Company] in Madrid.

[Company] is a Series C European B2B SaaS platform serving finance and operations teams across Europe. Around 12,000 customers, growing 30 percent year on year, backed by a tier-1 European fintech investor syndicate. The Madrid Sales Hub is the SDR engine for every European market in one office.

For someone joining the SDR team, this means you would own your pipeline from early on, cold to qualified, with AEs sat next to you reviewing what is converting and what is not.

Would you be open to me sharing the job description and compensation for this role?

#### Touch 2: The Contrarian Frame

Subject: (no subject, threads with Touch 1)

Hi [First Name], looping back on the SDR seat at [Company] in Madrid.

Most SDR seats in European B2B SaaS run on dial count and a single market. This one is the inverse: outcome-led, pan-European, where the patterns that work in one segment surface across all of them fast because every European market is on the same floor.

Can I share the job description and compensation for this role?

#### Touch 3: Day-to-Day Reality

Subject: [Company] - SDR | outcome-led floor (Madrid Sales Hub)

Hi [First Name], looping back on the SDR role at [Company]. Worth sharing how the floor actually operates.

You would report to the Sales Manager for the Madrid Hub. Around 25 SDRs on the floor cover all European markets, with around 5 AEs per region sat alongside them. Gong call reviews are standard, and AEs feed back directly on the meetings you book, so you find out within days whether your qualification work is holding up.

Compensation is structured for an SDR moving toward AE within 12 to 18 months, with OTE upside on qualified meetings. Full specifics on a call.

Would you be open to a 15-minute chat with the team?

#### Touch 4: Why Now and Honest Close

Subject: [Company] - SDR | last note (Madrid Sales Hub)

Hi [First Name], final note on the SDR role at [Company].

The Madrid Hub is still scaling. New joiners typically own their own book by week three or four, with AEs across every European market sat alongside them. Joining now means you would be in the pod while it is still being shaped, not slotting into a fully formed playbook.

That kind of foundational ownership disappears once the systems are built.

If the timing is not right, I would genuinely appreciate any feedback on the outreach. Even a one-liner helps us get better.

Director, Strategic Enterprise Partnerships at a European adtech scale-up at 9-figure ARR, backed by a major European media group. Greenfield seat with GM scope on a single named US partner and a planned joint venture.

[Company] is a European adtech scale-up backed by a major European media group, at 9-figure annual revenue and on the road to $500m. The role is a brand-new seat with GM scope on a single named US partner and a planned JV. The expansion of scope is at the partner level, not in seniority.

Would you be open to me sharing the job description and compensation for this role?

#### Touch 2: The Contrarian Frame

Subject: (no subject, threads with Touch 1)

#### Touch 3: Day-to-Day Reality

Subject: [Company] - Director, Strategic Partnerships | JV scoping now

Compensation: $180k to 210k base, OTE up to $240k, weighted toward base (70/30). Honestly, this aligns with senior strategic AM at adtech scale-ups, not a significant cash increase. The primary draw is the scope, the JV opportunity, and the parent group platform.

Would you be open to discussing next steps?

#### Touch 4: Why Now and Honest Close

Several people have asked what makes the timing on this seat unique. To be candid: the playbook does not exist yet. Strategic AM roles in adtech typically come with a well-established playbook three iterations deep. This one starts from scratch.

If you join at this point, you would be working directly with the MD and co-founders as the JV terms are drafted, not inheriting it post-legal approval.

If timing is off, I would genuinely appreciate any feedback on the outreach. Even a one-liner helps us get better.

Subject: Designing systems nobody has built before | [Company] - Staff Software Engineer
