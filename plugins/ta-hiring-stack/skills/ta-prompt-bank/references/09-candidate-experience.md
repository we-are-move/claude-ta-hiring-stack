### 49. Write a Decline Message that Builds Advocacy
**For:** Recruiter · **Time:** 5 min · **Output:** Email · **Wedge prompt**

**Inputs to have ready:** Candidate context (stage they reached, standout moments); real reason for decline. Stronger with: specific feedback from interviewers; a relevant resource (article, event, community) to share.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: A decline message that leaves the candidate genuinely well-disposed toward your company — and likely to recommend others, even though you said no.

TASK: Write a Decline Message that Builds Advocacy for the candidate whose context I will paste below.
Length: 150-200 words.
Format: 4 short paragraphs.

BRIEF
- Output: 150-200 words.
- Sounds like: a thoughtful colleague telling someone honestly. Warm, specific, never patronising.
- Success means: they do not feel bruised. They might recommend a friend. They might apply again in 18 months.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always reference something specific from their interview or background — not "we enjoyed meeting you" but something real.
- Always be honest about why — without being brutal.
- Always include a useful resource (a piece of content, an event, a community) that adds genuine value.
- Always include an opt-in CTA (newsletter, talent network) — never an opt-out.
- Always leave the door open naturally — but never use phrases like "we will keep your CV on file" unless you mean it.

CANDIDATE CONTEXT:
- Stage they got to: [first call / interview / final / offer-rejected]
- Notes / standout moments: [paste]
- Reason for decline (your real reason): [paste]

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).
```

**Sample output:**

> Hi Sarah,
>
> Thanks for the time you put into the process — particularly the merchant dashboard exercise on Tuesday. The way you framed the activation drop-off as a "moments of friction, not flow" problem is something a few of us are still thinking about.
>
> I am writing to let you know we have decided to move forward with another candidate for the Senior Product Designer role. The deciding factor was specific experience leading craft without a senior designer above — the other candidate has been the most senior designer on craft for the last three years, and we need someone who can step into that immediately without ramp-up. Your trajectory is clearly there, but we needed it now.
>
> Two things I think you would find genuinely useful: there is a brilliant essay by Ryan Singer on design ownership at Basecamp [link], and the Designers Guild London meet up the first Thursday of each month is where a lot of the strongest senior designers in B2B SaaS hang out.
>
> If you would like to hear about future roles that fit the level you are operating at, you can join our talent network here [link]. No pressure — and if not, all the best for what is next.

### 50. Build a Silver Medalist Re-engagement Sequence
**For:** Recruiter · **Time:** 30 min · **Output:** Sequence

**Inputs to have ready:** Candidate name + prior role + why declined; what you remember about them + new opportunity. Stronger with: ATS connector for relationship history.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: A 3-message warm re-engagement sequence for silver medalists — candidates declined for fit-with-this-role-now reasons, not absolute disqualifiers. Closer cadence and higher intimacy than general talent pool.

TASK: Build a Silver Medalist Re-engagement Sequence from the candidate context below.
Length: 800-1,100 words.
Format: 5 sections —
1. Tagging discipline — silver medalist vs general talent pool
2. Message 1 — direct reach-out from the original recruiter/HM
3. Message 2 — value-added (+10 days)
4. Message 3 — opportunity (+14 days)
5. Cadence + handling

BRIEF
- Sounds like: a recruiter who remembers the candidate as a person, not a database entry.
- Success means: 30-40% engagement rate; one converts to real conversation per quarter.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always reference the prior process specifically.
- Always send from the original recruiter or HM.
- Always lead Message 1 with the prior connection, never the new role.
- Always cap at 3 messages over ~25 days.
- Always include an explicit opt-out in every message.

CONVERSATION: only ask if you don't have prior-process context paste-ready.

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Candidate name + prior role/process: [paste]
- How far they got and why declined (fit-now, not absolute-no): [paste]
- What you remember about them as a person: [paste]
- The new opportunity: [paste]
```

**Sample output:** (extract — M1)

> Subject: "Catching up after last March"
>
> "Sarah — I think about your final interview last March quite often. The 'moments of friction, not flow' framing of the merchant dashboard problem stuck. We've genuinely referenced it in a couple of design conversations since.
>
> I'm not writing about a specific role today — just wanted to reconnect, see how the past 9 months have gone, and ask one question.
>
> What's the most interesting backend problem you've worked on this year?
>
> No pressure to reply. Just curious." *(82 words)*
>
> **Cadence.** If they engage at any point: respond same day, switch to natural conversation, abandon the rest of the sequence. After M3: mark "silver medalist contacted, revisit in 6 months." Never run the sequence twice in 12 months — that's harassment.

### 51. Build a Talent Pool Nurture Sequence
**For:** Recruiter / Head of TA · **Time:** Workflow · **Output:** Sequence

**Inputs to have ready:** Talent pool composition; company news + helpful resources. Stronger with: existing brand voice samples; persona insights to angle the content.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: A long-term nurture sequence for your general talent pool — candidates who applied speculatively or dropped early. Value-led, opt-out always, never promises future work.

TASK: Build a Talent Pool Nurture Sequence from the inputs below.
Length: 1,200-1,500 words.
Format: 6 sections —
1. The principles — what this sequence is and is not
2. Cadence — quarterly or monthly, with reasoning
3. The content mix — what to share
4. The 4-quarter content plan
5. Tagging + segmentation
6. Opt-out + measurement

BRIEF
- Sounds like: a TA leader who treats the talent pool as a long-term audience.
- Success means: engagement above industry benchmark; opt-out rate low; the pool produces inbound interest when roles open.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always lead with value, never with the company's openings.
- Always include an opt-out in every send.
- Always avoid promising future work or roles.
- Always avoid generic company updates.
- Always tag opt-outs and never re-add.
- Always segment if you can.

CONVERSATION: only ask if you don't have a sense of who's in the pool.

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Talent pool composition (functions, levels, geographies): [paste]
- Company news + helpful resources to share: [paste]
- Existing brand voice samples: [paste]
- (Optional) Previous nurture attempts: [paste]
```

**Sample output:** (principles + "no" list)

> **Principles.**
> 1. Lead with value, never with the company.
> 2. Never promise future work.
> 3. Avoid generic company news.
> 4. Always include a one-click opt-out.
> 5. Segment by function.
> 6. Quarterly cadence by default.
>
> **The "we will not do" list.**
> - Generic company updates ("we just raised our Series E!")
> - "Hope you're well" filler
> - "Just checking in to see if you'd be open to opportunities"
> - Re-adding opt-outs to "different" lists
> - Pretending the email is personal when it's a broadcast
>
> **Content mix.** Market intelligence (40%) · sector commentary (30%) · resource sharing (20%) · behind the scenes (10%).

**Related prompts:** #50 Build a Silver Medalist Re-engagement Sequence — the higher-intimacy companion · #49 Write a Decline Message that Builds Advocacy — where candidates opt into this pool

### 52. Run a Withdrawal Pattern Analysis
**For:** Head of TA · **Time:** 30 min · **Output:** Audit

**Inputs to have ready:** Withdrawal summaries + hiring funnel volumes. Stronger with: candidate NPS data; comparable benchmark data from the sector.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: Patterns from recent candidate withdrawals + what they say about your process, brand, or experience + the top 3 changes to make this quarter, ranked by impact × effort.

TASK: Run a Withdrawal Pattern Analysis from the withdrawal data below.
Length: 1,000-1,300 words.
Format: 5 sections —
1. Pattern summary — dominant withdrawal reason and secondary
2. Stage-by-stage analysis
3. Cause interpretation
4. The 3 priority changes for this quarter
5. What to measure to know if the changes worked

BRIEF
- Sounds like: a senior TA leader who treats withdrawals as the most valuable signal in hiring.
- Success means: by next quarter, the top 2 withdrawal patterns are measurably reduced.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always quantify.
- Always name the structural cause.
- Always include market context.
- Always include actions you control vs actions you don't.
- Always rank the 3 changes by impact × effort.

CONVERSATION: do not execute yet. Confirm: do you have stated reasons from candidates, or only stage data?

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Withdrawal summaries — 10+ recent cases: [paste]
- Hiring funnel volumes for the period: [paste]
- (Optional) Candidate NPS data: [paste]
```

**Sample output:** (extract)

> **Q3 — 15 withdrawals across 5 searches.**
>
> Dominant: 7/15 (47%) post-screening, before interview. Stated reason in 5 of 7: competing offer that moved faster. Implicit in the other 2: time-to-interview >14 days.
>
> Secondary: 4/15 (27%) at offer stage. Reason in 3 of 4: equity below market.
>
> **3 priority changes.**
> 1. *Compress screen-to-interview to <7 days.* Block HM calendars 2-3 hours/week. Impact: high. Effort: low. 30-day fix.
> 2. *Add equity refresh policy for candidates from Series E+ companies.* 0.15% refresh at 18 months in offer letter. 60-day fix.
> 3. *Run a 1-question exit survey with the 7 post-screen withdrawals.* "What did the other offer have that we didn't?" 7-day exercise.

### 53. Build a Candidate NPS Survey
**For:** Head of TA · **Time:** 5 min · **Output:** Survey

**Inputs to have ready:** Hiring volume per quarter. Stronger with: existing employee NPS questions to align style.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: An 8-12 question post-process survey to measure candidate experience — with action thresholds.

TASK: Build a Candidate NPS Survey from the inputs below.
Length: 800-1,100 words.
Format: 5 sections —
1. The principles
2. The survey questions — 8-12 questions across 4 themes (process, comms, fairness, employer perception)
3. Question design — scale, format, time-to-complete, anonymity
4. Distribution mechanics
5. Action thresholds

BRIEF
- Sounds like: a TA leader who treats NPS as a system not a vanity metric.
- Success means: response rate above 30%, the data drives a change quarterly.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always limit to 8-12 questions.
- Always include both quant scales and one open-text per theme.
- Always send after decision is communicated.
- Always treat survey as anonymous in design.
- Always define action thresholds in advance.

CONVERSATION: only ask if the organisation already runs an employee NPS — match the format.

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Hiring volume per quarter: [paste]
- Existing candidate NPS or survey: [paste]
- Existing employee NPS questions: [paste if applicable]
```

**Sample output:** (extract)

> **Process (Q1-3).** Q1: "On a scale of 1-10, how would you rate the overall application and interview experience?" Q2: "Was the interview process well-structured? (1 = chaotic, 10 = excellent structure)." Q3: How long did it take vs expected?
>
> **Employer perception (Q9-11).** Q9: "How likely are you to recommend [Company] as a place to work to a friend? (0-10)" (NPS). Q10: "How likely are you to apply to [Company] again? (0-10)." Q11: Open-text: "What's the single thing you'd want a friend considering this company to know?"
>
> **Action thresholds.** NPS <0: process audit (#52 + #11) within 30 days. Process score <6: HM calendar discipline review. Fairness score <7: Debiased Screening review (#35). Single substantive complaint: read within 48h.

### 54. Run a GDPR / EEO Hiring Comms Audit
**For:** Head of TA · **Time:** 30 min · **Output:** Audit

**Inputs to have ready:** Comms templates currently in use; jurisdiction(s) you hire in; retention policy. Stronger with: legal/People team input on data retention policy.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: An audit of your standard candidate communications against GDPR and EEO requirements — acknowledgements, rejections, retention, withdrawal rights — with specific remediations for any gaps.

TASK: Run a GDPR / EEO Hiring Comms Audit from the templates below.
Length: 1,000-1,400 words.
Format: 5 sections —
1. Jurisdiction summary
2. Template-by-template audit
3. Risk register
4. Remediations — priority order
5. Retention policy check

BRIEF
- Sounds like: a TA-leader-meets-data-protection-officer. Specific, never paranoid.
- Success means: comms are compliant; you can defend retention and deletion practices.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always specify jurisdiction clearly.
- Always treat retention as the highest-risk area.
- Always include a candidate-facing data deletion path.
- Always recommend changes a TA leader can make without legal review for low-risk items.
- Always check the consent mechanic at top of funnel.

CONVERSATION: do not execute yet. Ask: what jurisdictions do you hire in?

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Comms templates currently in use: [paste]
- Jurisdiction(s): [paste]
- Retention policy: [paste if any]
- (Optional) Past data subject access requests: [paste]
```

**Sample output:** (extract)

> **UK + EU.** UK GDPR + Data Protection Act 2018. Lawful basis required (typically "legitimate interests"); explicit consent for retention beyond hiring decision; right to erasure; DSAR within 30 days.
>
> **High risk.**
> 1. *Indefinite retention.* Many TA functions retain candidate data forever. Default should be 12 months for unsuccessful candidates.
> 2. *Implicit marketing consent.* Adding candidates to nurture sequences without explicit opt-in.
>
> **Remediations.**
> 1. *Set retention policy at 12 months.* Implement deletion automation in ATS within 30 days.
> 2. *Add retention + deletion language to every candidate-facing template.* 7-day fix.
> 3. *Decouple decline message content from structured rationale.* Decline is warm; scorecard is the legal record. 14-day fix.
> 4. *Add explicit opt-in for talent pool nurture (#51).* 30-day fix.
