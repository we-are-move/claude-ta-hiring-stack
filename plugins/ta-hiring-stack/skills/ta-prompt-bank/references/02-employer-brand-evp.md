# Stage 02 — Employer Brand & EVP

Prompts for building persona-driven messaging, general and role-level EVPs, inclusive employer brand content, and auditing employer brand health — all anchored in real employee voice.

### 7. Build a Persona Messaging Framework
**For:** Head of TA / Recruiter · **Time:** 30 min · **Output:** Framework

**Inputs to have ready:** JD and ICP for the role. Stronger with: Company stage and culture context.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: A 3-persona interview framework you can run as 15-20 minute coffee chats with current employees — to mine authentic stories that fuel recruitment outreach for the role you're hiring.

TASK: Build a Persona Messaging Framework from the role inputs below.
Length: 1,200-1,800 words.
Format: 5 sections —
1. Purpose & principle (story-mining, not assessment)
2. The 3 personas — for each: criteria (tenure, status, perspective, objective)
3. Per-persona question sets — 5-8 core questions with 2-layer follow-ups
4. Outputs to capture — quotable lines, headlines, comparisons, future hooks
5. Practical tips — agenda, time management, tone

BRIEF
- Output: 1,200-1,800 words, ready to send to whoever's running the interviews.
- Sounds like: a recruitment marketing strategist briefing a peer.
- Success means: the person running the interviews can sit down with three employees this week and walk away with usable raw material.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always tailor questions to the actual role and ICP — never generic.
- Always include 2-layer follow-ups: detail/example, then stretch/story.
- Always design questions to produce quotable, emotional, story-like answers.
- Always cover three pull dimensions: company-level, role-level, persona-level.
- Always frame interviews as informal coffee chats, never assessments.

CONVERSATION: do not execute yet. Confirm the role and ICP in one paragraph.

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Role being hired: [title + level + function]
- Ideal Candidate Profile (ICP): [current title, exp, skills, target companies]
- JD draft: [paste if available]
- Company stage + culture context: [paste if available]
```

**Sample output:** (Persona 1 extract)

> **Persona Messaging Framework — Senior Backend Engineer, Series C SaaS**
>
> *Persona 1 — New Joiner.* Backend Engineer joined in the last 3-6 months. Objective: capture decision triggers and early excitement.
>
> *Q1 — The Hook.* "When you were deciding to join, what was the one thing that really made you think 'yes, I want to work here'?"
> Follow-up 1 (contrast): "Was that different from what other companies were offering?"
> Follow-up 2 (stretch): "If you had to sum that feeling up in a headline, what would it say?"
>
> *Q2 — Early Surprise.* "What's been the biggest positive surprise since joining?"
> Follow-up 1: "Was there a specific moment that made you feel that?"
> Follow-up 2: "How would you describe it to another Backend Engineer to make them want to join?"

### 8. Write Messaging Assets from Persona Interview Notes
**For:** Recruiter · **Time:** 30 min · **Output:** Document

**Inputs to have ready:** Persona interview transcripts or notes. Stronger with: Existing brand voice samples.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: Messaging assets extracted from raw persona interview notes and ready to use in outreach.

TASK: Write Messaging Assets from the persona interview notes I'll paste below.
Length: 1,000-1,500 words across the four output sections + draft assets.
Format: 4 sections —
1. Quotable lines (8-12 raw quotes worth keeping — verbatim, with persona tag)
2. Headlines (5-8 one-line summaries)
3. Comparisons (4-6 "different from other companies because…" lines)
4. Future hooks (4-6 lines about what's exciting in the next 12 months)
PLUS: Draft outreach assets — 3 subject lines, 1 body paragraph, 2 CTAs.

BRIEF
- Sounds like: a recruitment marketing strategist mining gold from raw notes.
- Success means: every line is verbatim or close to it; outreach assets are ready to test.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always preserve raw wording where possible — energy comes from real speech.
- Always tag each output with which persona it came from.
- Always rank within each section — strongest first.
- Always include the source quote alongside the derived headline/comparison/CTA.

CONVERSATION: do not execute yet. Skim the notes; tell me which 2-3 themes are dominant.

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Persona interview transcripts or notes: [paste]
- Existing brand voice samples: [paste if available]
- Role being marketed: [title + level + function]
```

**Sample output:** (top 5 quotables + draft assets)

> **Quotable lines (top 5).**
> 1. *"I'd been bored for two years. The first month here, I was learning something new every day — and I'd been doing this for nine years."* — Seasoned Pro
> 2. *"The thing that made me say yes was they let me see the actual codebase before the offer."* — New Joiner
> 3. *"My manager has never once asked me for an update. She trusts the work to surface itself."* — High Performer
> 4. *"We move faster than any team I've been on — because we don't have to fight to make decisions."* — Seasoned Pro
> 5. *"Five rounds and not one was a take-home test."* — New Joiner
>
> **Draft outreach.**
> *Subject line A:* "Five-round interview, no take-home."
> *Body:* "Most companies hire engineers and ask them to trust the codebase is good. We don't. Every candidate we move past round two sees the actual codebase before the offer."

### 9. Build a General EVP
**For:** Head of TA · **Time:** Workflow · **Output:** Framework

**Inputs to have ready:** Employee research, Glassdoor reviews, existing careers page content. Stronger with: Web search for competitor career pages.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: A full General Employee Value Proposition — 80% reality and 20% best-day aspiration. Ready to anchor careers page, JDs, outreach and internal comms.

TASK: Build a General EVP from the inputs below.
Length: 1,500-2,000 words.
Format: 6 sections —
1. Core positioning statement (60-90 words, first person plural)
2. EVP pillars (3-5 qualities that define the offer)
3. The seven EB core areas — strengths and gaps across Company / Leadership / Culture / Citizenship / Career Catalyst / Rewards / Flexibility
4. The five EVP components — Financial Rewards, Employment Benefits, Career Development, Work Environment, Company Culture
5. The "give and get" — what you ask, what you offer
6. Voice and tone guidance — how the EVP should sound across channels

BRIEF
- Sounds like: a senior employer brand strategist who's honest with the client.
- Success means: 80% recognisable on a normal day + 20% who you are at your best. If you can't deliver, it doesn't make the cut.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always anchor in authenticity — pull language from real employee research.
- Always differentiate — the EVP should fail the "could a competitor say this?" test.
- Always identify gaps as well as strengths.
- Always use the 80/20 rule.
- Always make each pillar evidenceable.

TOOLS: web search for competitor career pages and EVPs.

CONVERSATION: do not execute yet. List 3 EB core areas you think are strongest, and 1-2 that need honest acknowledgement of weakness.

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Employee research (survey, eNPS, exit interview themes): [paste]
- Glassdoor reviews — last 12 months: [paste]
- Existing careers page content: [paste]
- Business context — stage, headcount, positioning: [paste]
```

**Sample output:** (positioning + pillars)

> **Core positioning.**
> *"We're a Series C company that values craft over career theatre. We hire people who want to do the work without fighting to make decisions about it. In return, we offer top-quartile pay, real architectural ownership, and the trust to ship without a committee."*
>
> **EVP pillars.**
> 1. *Craft over career theatre.* Proof: 70% of directors promoted internally; no "managing up" workshops.
> 2. *Decisions move at the speed of conviction.* Proof: average time from problem to ship is under 14 days.
> 3. *Compensation that doesn't make you check the market.* Proof: comp regrets in exit interviews below 5%.
> 4. *Time as the rarest resource.* Proof: average engineer has 22 hours of focus time per week.

### 10. Build a Role-Level EVP from Incumbent Interviews
**For:** Head of TA / Recruiter · **Time:** Workflow · **Output:** Framework

**Inputs to have ready:** Incumbent interview notes for the role. Stronger with: Web search for competitor JDs at the same level.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: A Role-Specific EVP, Role Pitch, and Outreach Hook for the role you are hiring — built from your incumbents.

TASK: Build a Role-Level EVP from the incumbent interview notes I'll paste below.
Length: 1,000-1,500 words.
Format: 3 deliverables —
1. Role-Specific EVP (200-300 words) — first-person plural articulation
2. Role Pitch (150-200 words) — verbal pitch usable in outreach or interview opening
3. Outreach Hook (3 variations, 30-50 words each)

BRIEF
- Sounds like: an incumbent talking to their friend at another company about why they should jump.
- Success means: a strong candidate recognises something true that competitors aren't articulating.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always anchor in incumbent language — pull verbatim where possible.
- Always be specific about projects, tools, decision rights, growth opportunities.
- Always state what makes this role uniquely compelling — not the company generically.
- Always position for a narrow audience.
- Always be honest — if a part is hard, name it.

TOOLS: web search for competitor JDs at the same level.

CONVERSATION: do not execute yet. List the 3 things incumbents say make this role unique.

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- Incumbent interview notes: [paste]
- Role being hired: [title + level + function]
- Competitor companies hiring similar roles: [list]
```

**Sample output:** (Role-Specific EVP)

> *"Senior Backend Engineers here own real architectural decisions — not just the implementation of decisions someone else made. You'll work on a platform processing 12k transactions per second, with the autonomy to refactor the bits you think need refactoring and the trust to take that decision without a committee. Two of our last three Senior promotions came because the engineer rewrote a subsystem we didn't know needed rewriting. Pay is at the 75th percentile of London. The interview process is five rounds, no take-home tests, and you'll see the actual codebase before the offer. If you've been bored in your current Senior seat — if the decisions you're making feel small — this is probably where you want to be."*

### 11. Build Inclusive Employer Brand Content
**For:** Head of TA · **Time:** Workflow · **Output:** Plan

**Inputs to have ready:** DE&I data, ERG leads, existing brand voice samples. Stronger with: Employee story submissions or interview snippets.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: A 12-month inclusive employer brand content plan that authentically represents underrepresented experiences within your company — without falling into performative tokenism.

TASK: Build an Inclusive Employer Brand Content plan from the inputs below.
Length: 1,500-2,000 words.
Format: 6 sections —
1. Audit summary — what your current content does well, where you're most exposed to "performative" criticism
2. Principles — 5-7 rules that govern what you will and won't produce
3. Content pillars (3-5) — each rooted in a real experience inside your company
4. 12-month content calendar — specific pieces with format, channel, owner, voice
5. The "no" list — content types that look inclusive but actually aren't
6. Risk and review process

BRIEF
- Sounds like: a DE&I-literate brand strategist briefing a CEO who wants to do this well but is afraid of doing it badly. Honest, never sanctimonious.
- Success means: every piece is grounded in a real experience, has a named voice attached, would pass scrutiny from someone with sharp DE&I literacy.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always anchor content in a specific employee experience, with that person's consent.
- Always credit the voice; never composite "an employee said…"
- Always coincide DE&I content with actual organisational change.
- Always include the messy bits — what didn't work, what's still hard.
- Always run sensitive content past an ERG before publishing.

CONVERSATION: do not execute yet. Confirm: do you have ERGs / affinity groups active, and is leadership committed to ongoing structural change?

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS
- DE&I data: [paste]
- ERG / affinity group leads: [paste]
- Existing brand voice samples: [paste]
- Recent inclusive content attempts: [paste]
- Company stage, sector, geographies: [paste]
```

**Sample output:** (principles + "no" list)

> **Principles.**
> 1. Every piece has a real, named voice attached. No composite quotes.
> 2. We never publish inclusive content without coincident structural change.
> 3. We publish the messy bits.
> 4. Calendar months don't drive content — they punctuate it.
> 5. ERG leads have editorial veto on content about their community.
>
> **The "no" list.**
> - Stock-photo diversity grids on the careers page.
> - Anonymous composite "an employee said" quotes.
> - Standalone calendar-month posts with no structural change to communicate alongside.
> - "Day in the life" content from a single high-performer that papers over structural friction.

### 12. Run an Employer Brand Audit
**For:** Head of TA · **Time:** Playbook (4-6 hours prep + follow-up review cycle) · **Output:** Audit

**Inputs to have ready:** Careers page URL, Glassdoor URL, top 3 competitors, sample JDs, recent content. Stronger with: Application drop-off data; recent eNPS or engagement data.

**The prompt:**

```
GOAL: A diagnostic audit of your current Employer Brand across the seven EB core areas, with a competitor Glassdoor benchmark, a walk-through of your own application process, and a prioritised set of fixes ranked by impact × effort.

TASK: Run an Employer Brand Audit from the inputs below.
Length: 2,500-3,500 words.
Format: 7 sections —
1. Executive summary — overall EB health score (1-4 per core area), top 3 priorities
2. Seven core areas scored 1-4
3. Competitor benchmark — top 3 competitors' Glassdoor scores, careers pages, EB campaigns
4. Application process walk-through — mobile + desktop, friction-point by friction-point
5. Current content audit
6. Top 3 priority fixes — ranked by impact × ease, with 30/60/90 day actions
7. Resourcing implications

LABEL: Playbook-tier audit. 4-6 hours of preparation + follow-up review cycle.

BRIEF
- Sounds like: an experienced employer brand consultant — diagnostic, honest, prioritised.
- Success means: Head of TA can present the top 3 fixes and get budget/resource agreement.

RULES (every rule is a TO DO)
- Always score 1-4: 1 = ad-hoc, 2 = repeatable, 3 = managed, 4 = optimised.
- Always include evidence for every score.
- Always benchmark against named competitors, not generic "industry."
- Always walk the application process yourself — note where you'd give up.
- Always rank fixes by impact × ease.
- Always name what's working alongside what isn't.

TOOLS: web search for competitor careers pages, Glassdoor, recent EB campaigns.

CONVERSATION: do not execute yet. List the 5 things you most need to score this credibly.

PUSH: go beyond the basics. Think before answering (maximum reasoning).

INPUTS (this prompt benefits from preparation)
- Your careers page URL: [paste]
- Your Glassdoor profile URL: [paste]
- Top 3 competitor companies: [paste]
- Sample of current JDs (3-5): [paste]
- Recent LinkedIn/careers content: [paste]
- Application drop-off data: [paste]
```

**Sample output:** (executive summary)

> **Employer Brand Audit — Q3.**
>
> **Overall EB health: 2.4 / 4** (Repeatable, edging toward Managed). Above average for a Series C. Culture (3.2) and Career Catalyst (3.0) differentiated from competitors. Citizenship (1.5) and Rewards transparency (1.8) pulling down. Glassdoor 4.1 vs top competitor's 3.7. App process loses ~38% mobile candidates (industry median 22%).
>
> **Top 3 priority fixes (90 days).**
> 1. **Mobile application journey.** Cut screening questions from 14 to 5, add progress indicator. Impact: very high. Effort: low. 30-day fix.
> 2. **Rewards transparency on JDs.** Publish comp ranges on every JD; one-page "how we pay" doc. 60-day fix.
> 3. **Citizenship content gap.** 4-piece quarterly series anchored to actual programmes. 90-day initiation.
>
> Resourcing: ~£12k tooling. ~8 weeks Head of TA time + 4 weeks Comms.
