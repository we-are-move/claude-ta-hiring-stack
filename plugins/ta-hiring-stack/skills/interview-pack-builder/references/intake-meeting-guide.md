# Intake Meeting Guide — The Hiring Bar Framework

Move, 2026

## Introduction

This guide provides a structured approach for conducting intake meetings with hiring managers. It is designed to help recruiters gather critical information about the business, team, and role, ensuring alignment on candidate requirements. The guide includes key questions, their rationale, and practical follow-up tips to dig deeper into responses.

> **Record and transcribe the meeting.** The transcript is the Hiring Bar Framework's primary input: a well-run 30 to 45 minute intake, recorded via Fathom, Granola, Otter, or a Zoom recording, gives the framework named managers, concrete team structures, and the hiring manager's actual language. Vague intakes produce vague packs. The framework will flag what the intake missed against the seven sections of this guide.

## 1. Role overview

### Questions

- Can you give me a brief overview of the role?
- Why is this role open? (New headcount or backfill?)
- What are the key projects they will be working on?
- What does success look like in this role within the first 3, 6, and 12 months?
- What are the key challenges someone in this role will face?
- Who does this role report to? Are there any direct reports?
- How does this role fit into the broader team and company objectives?

### Follow-up tips

- Clarify ambiguous responsibilities: "You mentioned ownership of X. Does this involve decision-making authority or more of a supporting role?"
- Push for impact: "What is the most important outcome this person should achieve in their first quarter?"
- Explore growth potential: "How do you see this role evolving over time?"

## 2. Team overview

### Questions

- What is the team's mission?
- What is the current team setup, and are there any upcoming changes?
- What cross-functional collaborations does this team engage in?
- What are the key skills or expertise gaps within the team that this hire will help fill?

### Follow-up tips

- Clarify vague or broad answers: "Could you give an example of how this mission translates into the day-to-day work for this team?"
- Probe into future vision: "You mentioned the product is expanding into [area]. How will this role contribute to that growth?"
- Link the team structure to recruitment needs: "What are the critical skills or gaps in the current setup that this hire will need to fill?"

## 3. Ideal candidate profile

### Questions

- Describe your ideal profile (are there any adjacent job titles?)
- How many years of experience? (Note: the framework will challenge this as a proxy; capture it anyway, it tells you how the HM thinks.)
- What are the must-have skills?
- What are the behaviours or traits a candidate needs to succeed in the role?
- Where will they be working right now? (Company size, stage, types of projects)
- What just won't work here?
- What will their route into the role have been? (Specific background preferred)
- How has AI changed this role on your team, and what do you expect a strong candidate's relationship with AI tools to look like?

### Follow-up tips

- Ask for real-world examples: "Can you share a past hire who embodied this ideal profile and what made them successful?"
- Clarify must-have versus nice-to-have: "If a strong candidate lacks X, what would make them still viable?"
- Challenge assumptions on experience: "Would you consider someone from a different industry with transferable skills?"

## 4. Performance measurement

### Questions

- What specific KPIs or outcomes indicate success in this role?
- What behaviours or work habits have caused past hires to struggle, and what was the impact?
- Who on your team sets the standard for performance, and what do they do differently?
- How does the team typically celebrate successes?
- What is the most common feedback you give to team members?
- Are there any rituals, events, or activities that the team regularly partakes in together?

### Follow-up tips

- Highlight team exemplars: "You mentioned [name of top performer]. What specific traits or skills do they bring that we should look for in a candidate?"
- Explore feedback culture: "You mentioned [most common feedback]. How do you usually communicate this, and what has been the response?"

## 5. Value propositions (messaging and USP)

### Questions

- What unique opportunities will a new hire have in this role?
- What specific moment or event made you realise, "Yes, I made the right decision to join here"?
- What are the growth opportunities in this role? (Professional development, upward mobility, cross-departmental collaborations)
- What are some exciting projects in the pipeline?
- What kind of technology, tools, or approaches is the team using that sets it apart?
- What is the long-term vision or goal for this team? And how does this role contribute to it?

### Follow-up tips

- Push for subject-matter-level insights, not lay-person insights.
- Push for specifics on growth opportunities: "You mentioned [growth opportunity]. Can you share an example of someone on your team who has experienced this firsthand?"
- Highlight exciting projects: "You shared [recent achievement]. What specific skills or contributions made that possible, and how will this role build on that success?"

## 6. Agreeing the hiring process

### Questions

- What are the key stages of the hiring process for this role?
- Who will be involved at each stage (hiring manager, engineers, people team)?
- What type of technical assessment will be used (live coding, take-home test, system design)?
- Will candidates be allowed, or expected, to use AI tools in assessments?
- How will culture and team fit be evaluated?

### Follow-up tips

- Assessments should reflect and evaluate on-the-job tasks and skills as closely as possible.
- Clarify assessment expectations: "What skills or competencies should be measured in the technical test, and how will success be defined?"

## 7. Ways of working

Goal: set clear expectations between recruiter and hiring manager to avoid delays and misalignment.

### Questions to cover

- Communication: how will we share updates (Slack, email, weekly call)?
- Update frequency: how often will we review the pipeline?
- Feedback turnaround: how quickly should interview feedback be given? (e.g. within 24 hours)
- Decision-making: who has final sign-off on candidates?
- Escalation: what is the process if there is a hiring block or delay?
- Collaboration: how can the recruiter best partner with the HM to ensure speed and quality?

### Follow-up tips

- Get commitments in writing and add them to the project plan.
- If multiple stakeholders, agree who is responsible for what at each stage.
- Keep it simple: one communication channel and clear feedback deadlines.
